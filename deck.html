The Problem

AI Arrived With No Introduction

Every website, API, service, device, and platform on the internet was built for humans. A human arrives, reads, interprets, decides. The resource doesn't need to explain itself — humans figure it out.

AI agents are now first-class consumers of the web. And they have a fundamental problem that no existing standard addresses:

Before I interact with this resource, what do I need to know about it? What is it? What am I allowed to do? Who is responsible for it? What must never happen?

An AI arriving at any resource today has no reliable way to answer these questions. It guesses. It infers. It hallucinates context. And when AI guesses wrong — that is where every AI safety incident, every agent misbehaviour, every autonomous system doing something it shouldn't, begins.

The same problem exists in the other direction. AI systems themselves have no standard way to introduce themselves to the resources they interact with. Platforms can't verify whether the entity arriving is legitimate, what it intends to do, or who is responsible for it.

Nothing has ever introduced itself. So nothing knows what it is dealing with.

The Solution

A Universal Declaration Standard — and the Infrastructure On Top of It

Layer 1 — The Introduction Layer Standard (Open, W3C)

A universal standard for how any entity — a web resource, an AI system, a device, a robot, a physical object — declares its identity, purpose, constraints, and responsible party before any interaction begins. Machine-native. Discoverable. Verifiable. The same schema in every direction.

A website introduces itself to AI systems. An AI system introduces itself to the platforms it interacts with. A robot introduces itself to the infrastructure it operates within. Everything speaks the same language, bidirectionally.

The standard is open. Deliberately — because a standard that captures a tax at the point of adoption never becomes universal. Open standards become infrastructure.

W3C Introduction Layer Community Group — officially announced August 10, 2026. Proposed with the backing of W3C leadership. Bryan Horsfield chairs the group.

Layer 2 — AILattice (The Business)

AILattice is the certification, registry, verification, and ranking infrastructure built on top of the open standard.

The DigiCert model applied to AI trust: SSL certificates are free to understand and implement. Verified, trusted SSL certificates — the ones systems actually trust — come from certificate authorities. DigiCert and Let's Encrypt built billion-dollar businesses on trust verification for a free open standard. AILattice is the certificate authority for the Introduction Layer.

The Google model applied to the AI web: Google built a $200B+ annual revenue business by being the index for human web navigation — ranking based on backlinks, keywords, and human behaviour signals. None of that works for AI agents. They don't follow links. They navigate by understanding what resources are and what they're allowed to do. AILattice is the ranking and index system for AI web navigation. Every AI agent that needs to navigate the web autonomously queries AILattice — not Google.
