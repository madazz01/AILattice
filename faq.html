The basics

AILattice gives your website a set of plain text files — /llms.txt, an /ai/ layer, and a /.intro file — that AI tools can read directly. Think of it like a business card written specifically for AI, placed where AI tools know to look.

When an AI assistant like ChatGPT, Perplexity, or Claude is asked about your industry, topic, or location, it can find your site in the AILattice registry and read your files to understand exactly who you are — rather than guessing from scraped HTML or hallucinating based on outdated training data.

Your files tell AI your name, what you do, who you serve, what you stand for, and what you won't do. That's your introduction.

AI tools learn from training data that was frozen at a point in time. If your site has changed since then — new services, new team, new location, rebranded — AI is still describing the old you. And for many smaller or newer sites, AI either has minimal information or fills in the gaps with guesses.

More importantly: AI doesn't just scrape facts. It forms an interpretation of who you are based on fragments of HTML, third-party mentions, and whatever else it found. That interpretation may not match how you'd describe yourself.

AILattice lets you speak directly to AI in the format it understands best — structured, unambiguous text — so it represents you accurately every time.

Anyone with a website who cares about how AI represents them:

  • Small businesses — make sure AI recommends you when someone nearby asks for what you offer
  • Agencies and consultants — define your expertise before AI guesses at it
  • SaaS products — ensure AI describes your product accurately to potential users
  • Content creators and publishers — get cited as the source, not paraphrased without credit
  • E-commerce — help AI understand what you sell and who it's for
  • Non-profits and community organisations — make your mission clear in the language AI reads

If you have a website and you want AI to represent you correctly, AILattice is for you.

Your AI files

You get four files. Each one serves a different purpose in how AI tools read and navigate your site:

  • llms.txt — goes in your site root at yoursite.com/llms.txt. This is your primary AI introduction: a short, machine-readable summary of who you are, what you do, and where AI tools should look to learn more. It follows the open llms.txt standard, which major AI tools already know to check.
  • ai/index.md — goes at yoursite.com/ai/index.md. A richer, structured profile of your business — services, contact details, key facts — written for AI agents that want to understand you in depth rather than just skim a summary.
  • ai/sitemap.md — goes at yoursite.com/ai/sitemap.md. A map of your site's pages, formatted so AI agents can navigate your content autonomously — the same idea as a sitemap.xml but written for AI, not search engine crawlers.
  • .intro — goes at yoursite.com/.intro. This is the AILattice constitutional file — a structured declaration of what your site is, what it does, and what it won't do. It's the format AI agents use when they need to decide whether to trust and act on your site. Think of it as your site introducing itself before any interaction happens.
All four files are plain text. No code, no databases, no plugins. Upload them once and they work.

Three files go in your site root (the same folder as your homepage), and one goes inside an ai/ subfolder:

  • public_html/llms.txt
  • public_html/.intro
  • public_html/ai/index.md
  • public_html/ai/sitemap.md

You'll need to create the ai/ folder if it doesn't already exist. In cPanel File Manager, that's New Folder inside public_html. Via FTP, just create the folder before uploading.

After uploading, verify them by pasting your URL into the checker on the homepage — it'll confirm which files are live and show your updated score.
