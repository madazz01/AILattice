The Manifesto

Why.

The introduction layer. Every website, device, and system — introduced before AI begins.

"I didn't set out to build a universal foundation. I just kept removing the roadblocks to creating my ideas, until I realised the roadblocks weren't unique to me."
— Bryan, Founder
Every system that acts on information first has to reconstruct what it means.
Artificial intelligence agents. Robots. Autonomous machines. APIs. Future systems we haven't built yet.
All of them start from fragments and infer the rest.
Most of that inference is unnecessary. The information already exists.
What if the information could declare itself?
The problem
The web was designed for storage, retrieval, and presentation.
Not for interpretation.
Every boundary in the current stack loses context.
Human → software.
Sensor → API.
Data → decision.
Website → agent.
At every crossing, meaning bleeds out.
We spend enormous effort translating across boundaries that should never have existed.
The missing layer isn't bigger models.
It's a shared interpretive frame.
We're not building another artificial intelligence tool.
We're building what AI — and everything else — reads first.
The foundational interpretation layer, that allows universal forward integration.
The architecture
An interpretation layer sits between information and action.
It doesn't process. It doesn't decide. It doesn't act.
It preserves intent across every boundary it crosses.
The mechanism is declaration.
Before any system reasons about you or acts on information from you — you declare exactly who you are, what you stand for, and what must never be violated.
That declaration is your introduction.
Not a document. Not metadata. A permanent introduction that any AI system inherits before it begins reasoning about you.
A website inheriting to an artificial intelligence agent. A sensor inheriting to an autonomous vehicle. A machine inheriting to a logistics system. A service inheriting to a future platform we haven't imagined yet.
Same layer. Same mechanism. Universal.
What forward integration means
Backward integration connects what already exists.
Forward integration prepares for what doesn't exist yet.
A declaration made today is inherited by systems built in five years.
An introduction filed now governs how an autonomous agent — not yet written — reasons about you when it first encounters you.
This is why the interpretation layer has to be foundational.
Standards are rarely obvious when they're first proposed.
SSL existed before browsers expected it.
Sitemaps existed before search engines depended on them.
Schema.org existed before structured data became commonplace.
We'd rather help build the layer early than wish it existed later.

From the builder
I didn't start with artificial intelligence. I started with a question about boundaries.
Every time information crosses a boundary — from sensor to system, from site to agent, from human to machine — something is lost. Context. Intent. Constraint. Meaning.
The web gave us a way to store and retrieve. We never built the layer that tells systems what the information means before they act on it.
That layer is what I've been trying to build. AI Lattice is the first visible proof of it — websites declaring their intent for AI agents to inherit. But the architecture was never just for websites, and it was never just for AI.
Whether AILattice becomes the standard isn't the point. Building the layer is.
